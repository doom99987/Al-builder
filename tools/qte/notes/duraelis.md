# duraelis / duraelis-comp: run log and check

The game's Duraelis boss QTE (IIFE "DURAELIS QTE" in js/qte.js, rules part `duraelis` in js/qte-rules.js). A puzzle, not a timing test: soul flames sit on a grid, every W/A/S/D press moves every soul one cell at once (wrapping at the edges), and they all have to sit on a spotlight when the round's timer runs out.

## Measured (owner's Medal clip, 1936x1056, 59.7 fps; the owner's description)
- The screen goes greyscale and misty: dark grey fog, soft white light pools. Souls are purple-blue flames with a white core, ~60 px.
- In the clip: a grid of 4 columns x 3 rows, cells ~159 px, ~636 x 477 px centred (souls only at x = 725/884/1043/1202, y = 410/572/725).
- Each W/A/S/D press moves EVERY soul one cell that way (lockstep); the step animates in ~0.1 s. Off an edge a soul wraps to the other side (seen: col 3 + D -> col 0).
- Spotlights: white beams from above landing as bright pools on cells. A grey bar at the bottom (track ~580 px) drains over the round. A round is not ended when solved: it runs until the bar empties ("the lights flare"); souls on a spotlight then are saved, the others turn grey and fade.
- Souls keep their cells from one round into the next. After a failed round the lost souls are gone and the next round may spawn new ones.
- Clip: an intro of ~2 s (one flame in the centre with "Guide every soul flame into a spotlight with WASD before the lights flare.", then it splits into 2 that fly to their cells), 6 rounds of 2.85 / 2.9 / 3.2 / 3.4 / 3.6 / 3.9 s with ~0.6 s between them, a big white flare, back to combat. Up to 2 souls. The bar got longer each round in the clip; the owner says each completion REDUCES the timer, and the trainer follows the owner.
- Owner: each completion makes the next harder and shorter; irregular completions add souls; 3 lives; the lives lost decide the damage taken and the benefits; flawless = 0 DMG and Duraelis stunned for 3 turns.

## Owner's changes after the first build (2026-10-18)
- **The grid is 4 x 4**, not the clip's 4 x 3: 16 cells, a 16-bit mask, and the shift is one of the 15 nonzero (dx 0..3, dy 0..3). A round can now need up to 4 presses (2 sideways + 2 up/down). The 15 shifts by fewest presses: 4 need one, 6 two, 4 three, 1 four.
- **A life per unlit flame** at the flare (two flames in the dark = two lives), never below 0; the run ends the moment the lives reach 0, so a round that loses three flames ends it at once. A round still counts as cleared only when every flame is lit; the unlit flames die and the lit ones carry on. The fight result counts lives lost the same way (in rounds 1-6, at most 3).
- **On-screen arrow keys on touch screens**: up, left, down, right as a keyboard's arrow cluster (up over left / down / right), under the board, 64 px (72 px from 420 px wide), shown the whole time the panel is open. The swipe on the board stays.

## Design (spec, as built)
s = rounds cleared this run.

| | casual | comp |
|---|---|---|
| round timer (game time, the bar) | `max(4.0 - 0.2 s, 2.0)` s | `max(3.4 - 0.2 s, 1.6)` s |
| souls in the round | `1 + #{1, 3, 6, 10, 15, 21 <= s}`, at most 6 | `2 + #{2, 4, 7, 11, 16 <= s}`, at most 7 |

- Because the souls move in lockstep on a torus their pattern never changes, so a round is solvable only if the spotlights are that pattern shifted. They are: a uniformly random NONZERO shift of the souls, so every round can be solved in at most 4 presses.
- Lives 3. At the flare: every soul lit = a clear (+1, all souls carry on); otherwise a life for each unlit soul (not below 0), the unlit souls die (grey, fading), the lit ones carry on. The next round starts 600 ms after the flare: it tops the souls up to the table's count on free cells (uniform), then draws the shift. Lives at 0 end the run. Score = rounds cleared (not consecutive).
- The first round starts 1.2 s after Start, with the game's instruction as the status line.
- Fight result (the game's QTE is 6 rounds): after round 6, a line under the board (under the arrow keys on touch screens) from the lives lost in rounds 1-6 - 0: "Flawless: 0 DMG taken, Duraelis stunned for 3 turns"; 1 or 2: "1 life lost: you take reduced damage" / "2 lives lost: you take reduced damage"; 3: "All lives lost: full damage" (shown when the run ends inside the first 6 rounds). The run goes on after round 6 for the board.
- Keys: W/A/S/D or the arrows, one step a keydown (`e.repeat` ignored), accepted any time in a round (the logic moves at once, the 0.1 s step is drawn). Outside a round they do nothing.
- Highscores in `alb:duraelis-hs` / `alb:duraelis-hs-comp`; new highs call `run.submit(v)`, a match reports to `_qteMatch.report()` and the run's end to `_qteMatch.fail()`; `alb-scores-reset` / `alb-mode-changed` as the other trainers.

## Choices beyond the spec (the clip could not show these)
- **Souls are a set of cells, logged as a 16-bit mask** (bit c, c = col + 4 row). Lockstep moves keep souls on distinct cells, so a soul needs no identity; the check replays masks.
- **Every draw is a logged uniform u at 4 dp.** A spawn's cell is the free cells (ascending)[floor(u f)], the shift is SHIFTS[floor(u 15)] in a fixed order (dy major, dx minor, skipping (0, 0)). u is rounded before it is used, so the check recomputes the game's own choice and runs KS tests on continuous draws.
- **The shift is uniform over the 15 nonzero shifts, as specified, even for a symmetric soul pattern.** Two souls two cells apart along a row, a column or both diagonals put the spotlights exactly on the souls for that shift: a free round (no press needed). About 1.3% of two-soul rounds (20% of pairs are such a pair, then 1 shift in 15), less with more souls. Excluding those shifts would change the spec's draw and its statistics, so it is left in; the check handles it (the fewest presses is taken over every offset that solves the board).
- **Game time is the sum of the frame dts, each clamped to [0, 50 ms], frozen while paused** (as sword-new); the flare is the first frame where it reaches the timer. The 600 ms gap and the 1.2 s intro are wall-clock timers; one that fires while paused starts its round on Resume.
- **The end:** the Start button comes back 1.5 s after the last flare (the flare and the dying souls fade meanwhile). A Start inside that time is not stopped by the old run's reset.
- **Keys are read by `e.key`** (w/a/s/d in either case, and the arrows), as fist and thorian read them: exactly the keys js/core.js's ping simulator delays (w/a/s/d by key, the arrows by code), and it keeps `e.repeat`. With Ctrl / Alt / Meta held a key does nothing (Ctrl+W/A/S/D are browser shortcuts). Focus in an input, textarea, select or contentEditable ignores them (the ping slider takes the arrows). The page's arrow-key scrolling is stopped only during a run.
- **The arrow keys (touch):** shown when `IS_MOBILE` is true (touch events or a coarse main pointer - the test the other trainers' TAP buttons use), or on a screen up to 1024 px wide with any coarse pointer or touch points. They show from the moment the panel opens and stay through the whole QTE (taps outside a round do nothing). A tap steps on `pointerdown` (no 300 ms click delay); the `touchstart` that follows only cancels it, so no click or mouse event follows and nothing fires twice; without pointer events they step on `touchstart`. `touch-action: none` on the keys and their box stops rapid taps from scrolling or zooming the page. Each tap flashes its key (`.pressed`, 120 ms, plus `:active`). The keys are inline SVG arrows (no font glyphs), styled in css/mobile.css. The board and its Start / Resume buttons sit in their own box (`.duraelis-qte-stage`), so Start is centred on the board and never on the keys; the result line sits under the keys, so its appearing after round 6 cannot move them under the player's thumb. Swipes of 24 px or more on the board step along their longer axis. Touch input does not go through the ping simulator (as every trainer's touch input).
- **The board is hidden while paused** (no souls, no spotlights): the round's clock stops while paused, so a visible board would be free thinking time. Pausing inside most rounds is also a review reason (below).
- **"Lit souls glow brighter" is live:** a soul sitting on a spotlight (once its step has finished) is drawn brighter, and in the gap after a clear all souls glow.
- **Look**, as the "drawn like the old ones" trainers - nothing but the QTE: a dark grey board with slowly drifting fog, a faint grid, each spotlight a soft white pool under a faint beam slanting down from the upper right, purple-blue flames with a white core, the grey timer bar under the board (orange in its last third), three small flames for the lives over the board's corner (grey when lost), the flare as a white flash that fades. The canvas is the board's width (900 px at most) and 62% of it tall on a desktop (at most 560 px, so the timer stays on a 900 px screen), 110% on touch screens; the board is the largest square that fits. The first round's souls fly out of the intro's centre flame in 0.25 s; later spawns fade in over 0.2 s. A wrap slides the soul out of one edge and in from the other (the soul is drawn on the torus and clipped to the board).
- **Texts:** the status line shows the game's instruction during the intro and every round (touch: "with the arrows"); "✓ Every soul reached the light!" after a clear, "✗ n souls lost to the dark. L lives left." after a fail, "All lives lost. Cleared N." at the end. The header shows "Cleared: N" (not "Streak": a fail does not reset it). The result line uses "1 life lost" for one (the owner wrote "<n> lives lost").
- **The fight result counts rounds played** (the 6th flare), not rounds cleared.
- **Labels:** sb.js `QTE_LABELS['duraelis'] = 'Duraelis'` (the same as the default capitalisation, set explicitly); matchmaking's list has it in the New group, so its (dormant) matchmaking label reads "Duraelis (New)".

## The log (QteRules.Run; t = ms since Start; numbers 4 dp)
| code | fields | where |
|---|---|---|
| `N` | the spawned soul's cell c, its draw u | startRound, before its R (one per new soul) |
| `R` | cleared s, lives, souls n, timer T (game ms), the souls' mask m after the spawns, the shift dx, dy, its draw u | startRound (the intro timer, the 600 ms timer, or Resume when that timer fired while paused) |
| `K` | key `W`/`A`/`S`/`D`, the round's game time g (ms), the souls' mask after the step | the key handler (after the ping delay), an arrow key, a swipe |
| `F` | game time g (>= T), the mask of the lit souls | gameLoop, the frame that ran the timer out |
| `E` | `'lives'` | right after the F that took the last life |
| `P` / `U` | paused / Resume clicked | pauseRun / resumeGame |

A round is 2-6 events of about 35 bytes. The log hits MAX_EVENTS (20,000) before MAX_BYTES: the suite's full log (comp, the fastest player) holds 4,849 rounds in 695 KB with the envelope, 3.0 hours of play. Later submits carry that full log; the check posts the rounds it proves without calling the higher claim invalid.

## What check() does
- Replays the state machine: intro, round, gap, over (no lives left: only E may follow), ended. Pauses are allowed in the intro, a round or the gap, never twice; U needs a P.
- `N`: only in the intro or a gap, not paused; the cell must be free (not a carried soul or an earlier spawn), the draw must pick exactly that cell, and no more souls than the round's count.
- `R`: the cleared count, the lives left, the soul count and the timer must be the table's for that count and mode, and the lives what the flares left. The mask must be exactly the carried souls (the last flare's lit souls, at their cells) plus this gap's spawns. The shift must be nonzero, in range and the one its draw picks. The first round no sooner than 1,200 - 120 ms after Start, every next one no sooner than 600 - 120 ms after the last flare.
- `K`: only in a round, not paused; a known key; the mask must be the previous mask moved by that key; g never goes back and stays under the timer (a drawn frame had it running).
- `F`: only in a round; g in [T, T + 50 ms); the lit mask must be exactly the souls on the spotlights. All lit: a clear; otherwise a life for each unlit soul, not below 0, and only the lit souls carry on. Lives at 0: the run is over.
- Clock: game time may not run more than GT_AHEAD = 200 ms ahead of the unpaused wall clock, since the round's R and since its previous press (as sword-new). The most seen in the page runs was 63 ms.
- Score = rounds cleared. A claim above it is invalid unless the log is full (MAX_EVENTS).
- Stats: rounds, clears, fails (rounds lost), soulsLost, lives, presses, pauses, pausedRounds, rtN/rtMedMs/rtMadMs, ivN/ivMadMs/ivSdMs, optN/optShare, shiftP, spawnP, easyShare/easyP, sameP, clockRatio, coarseClock, platformMismatch, logFull.

## Review thresholds (RV), and how they were calibrated
All of these hold for an admin; none rejects. The calibration run (4 x 4, a life per unlit flame) is `N=20000 NI=3000 NL=400 NB=40`: 39,984 honest checks (20,000 runs plus mid-run prefixes), 400 long runs by the best players, 3,000 page loads on the real IIFE and 40 bot runs - 0 invalid, 0 held. The simulated player sees the spotlights a frame after R, decides after a lognormal time (median 320-1,000 ms for one soul by skill, +8-16% a soul, +10% a press beyond the first, lapses x2-4), presses with lognormal gaps (median 90-210 ms), misreads the shift 1.5-20% of rounds (more with more souls), misses the wrap (DDD for A, SSS for W) 1-60%, picks either way round for two columns or rows, double-taps, sometimes jumps at a random key, and looks again after its keys land to fix the board.
- **First press too fast: the median from R to a round's first press < 150 ms over >= 10 rounds (RT_N 10, RT_MED 150).** A solver reading the board: 24-37 ms on the real client. The lowest honest median was 309 ms. It is loose on purpose: the round's start can be timed (600 ms after the flare), and a person reacting to the board appearing alone needs ~180 ms.
- **First presses too even: their MAD < 15 ms over >= 15 rounds.** A solver waiting a fixed 400 ms, or reading at once through the ping sim's 150 ms: a few ms. The lowest honest value was 44.5 ms.
- **Presses too even: within a round, press-to-press MAD < 4 ms AND SD < 8 ms over >= 20 intervals.** A key timer: MAD under 3 ms, SD under 5 ms. The lowest honest MAD was 8.9 ms and the lowest honest SD 13.4 ms. On a clock with a 16.67 ms grain (privacy modes) honest intervals can land on one or two grid values and collapse the MAD (an earlier 4 x 3 calibration saw 1.5 ms); the SD stays large, so the SD keeps such a player from being held.
- **A solver: every round solved with presses in the fewest presses, with the median first press < 250 ms, over >= 20 such rounds.** 690 honest runs were perfect over 20+ rounds; the fastest of them had a median first press of 349 ms.
- **KS on the draws, p < 1e-6: the shift draws over >= 20 rounds, the spawn draws over >= 7 spawns.** A run spawns only 6-7 souls plus the ones it loses (and runs are shorter with a life a flame), so 7 is the fewest draws at which seven equal ones fall under the alpha (2.9e-7; six give 2.3e-6). The lowest honest p were 3.2e-4 (shifts) and 1.3e-3 (spawns). Every spawn on the first free cell and a constant shift draw are held.
- **Cherry-picked shifts, binomial p < 1e-8 over >= 12 rounds: too many one-press shifts (4 of the 15), or one shift too often (x15 for the choice).** The lowest honest p were 6.7e-6 (one-press share) and 7.4e-5 (one shift). Thirty rounds of one-press shifts come out at 8e-11 by the KS alone and far lower by the binomial; one shift every round at 3e-18.
- **Game clock < 65% of the wall clock: the upper quartile over >= 8 rounds of the flare's g / the round's unpaused wall time.** Every round runs its whole timer, so every round gives a sample. A slow-motion client is slow in every round (x0.5 and x0.6 both held); a machine that stalls now and then is not. A 15 fps machine runs at 75% (67 ms frames clamped to 50); the lowest honest value was 0.700. Skipped, with the even-timing checks, when >= 90% of event times are multiples of 100 ms (Tor / resist-fingerprinting).
- **Paused inside >= 80% of the rounds, at least 10 of them.** The board is hidden while paused, but the round's clock stops. The highest honest share was 0.42.

## What it cannot catch
- A script with a person's timing - a lognormal first press around 450-650 ms, lognormal gaps between presses - that always plays the fewest presses, or that writes a whole log with fresh draws that way. The suite prints both as known limits: valid, full score. Only the SQL clock and record checks bound it. The puzzle's whole difficulty is reading the board; a log cannot show who read it.
- A solver that adds the ping simulator's delay or its own, varied, to every first press passes the first-press checks (the ping is not subtracted: the slider can move mid-run).
- Slow motion applied to `performance.now` and rAF together: the log is then self-consistent, only stretched. Slow motion above 65% speed also passes.
- On a 16.67 ms or 100 ms clock grain a key timer's rhythm is hidden (the even-timing checks need the SD, or are off).
- The mob flag and platform are only recorded (a stat). There are no mobile tables.

## For the merge (not done here)

Every point needs a whole round, whatever the grid or the lives: the fastest is a clear every round, so k points need 1.2 s + the first k timers + 0.6 s between them; per point that falls towards 2.0 + 0.6 = 2.6 s (casual) / 1.6 + 0.6 = 2.2 s (comp) from above, cut by a quarter. The 4 x 4 grid and the life-per-flame rule leave this unchanged. Run the function's `create or replace` in the SQL editor. supabase/qte-verified.sql needs nothing: the trainer id fits `^[a-z]+(-new)?(-comp)?$`, and a trainer with no record yet posts its first verified score.
